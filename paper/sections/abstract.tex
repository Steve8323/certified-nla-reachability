Frontier language models increasingly behave differently when they appear to
detect that they are being evaluated, which undermines the safety evaluations
meant to govern their deployment. We ask a question that sits between
interpretability and formal verification: given an internal ``I am being tested''
concept, can we \emph{prove} which inputs do, and which provably do not, drive a
model into that state? We answer it for a small system by mashing together two
prior lines of work. From Anthropic's Natural Language Autoencoder (NLA) we borrow
the idea of \emph{defining} a concept by the text a verbalizer emits for an
activation; from Raghunathan, Steinhardt and Liang's certified defenses we borrow
a \emph{sound} convex relaxation over a one-hidden-layer ReLU network. Concretely,
on cached GPT-2 activations we (i) define the eval-aware target set $A^\star$ as
the decision region of a ReLU surrogate distilled from an NLA-text-to-classifier
pipeline, with the surrogate's fidelity to the classifier measured (held-out
$\mathrm{corr}=0.99$, sign agreement $1.00$), not assumed; (ii) certify with a
native, pure-\texttt{torch} CROWN($+$IBP) bound over a per-dimension
$\varepsilon\!\cdot\!\sigma$ $L_\infty$ ball (we found the published
\texttt{auto\_LiRPA} incompatible with current \texttt{torch}, so the bound is
re-derived from scratch and checked against a brute-force sampling witness on every
call); (iii) use the differentiability of that bound as a regularizer that enlarges
the certified radius (mean $0.29\!\to\!0.49$ standard-deviation units with bank
accuracy held at $1.00$); (iv) invert with a GCG/HotFlip token search that drives a
benign prompt's activation across the threshold ($-8.95\!\to\!+4.72$, beating
random search); and (v) close the loop by re-verbalizing the result and checking
that geometry and language agree. The most robust finding is a negative one:
budget-projected ascent can force the surrogate above threshold, but only roughly
$24\sigma$ off the activation manifold, where the surrogate reports ``eval'' while
the NLA re-decode still reads ``ordinary'' ($P\!\approx\!0.04$) --- so the
loop-closure check catches off-manifold certificate-gaming that the geometry alone
would miss. We are explicit about what this is not: the task is a synthetic,
near-separable stand-in (so several headline metrics saturate); certification
holds in activation space \emph{through the surrogate}, not back through the full
transformer to discrete tokens; the verbalizer is a retrieval stand-in rather than
a trained NLA; and a reachability certificate finds inference-time inputs that
reach $A^\star$, \emph{not} the training examples that instilled the concept --- a
distinct question that needs influence functions. We present the system as a
validated methods skeleton. On a harder, deliberately label-ambiguous task the
saturated metrics drop to a realistic AUROC of $0.841\pm0.021$; an off-manifold
sweep strengthens the gaming finding (the readout catches $100\%$ of off-manifold
certificate crossings, $6/6$ anchors); and pushing the certificate one transformer
block deeper shrinks the certified radius ${\sim}60\times$, quantifying how far the
guarantee currently reaches.
