\section{Conclusion}

We asked whether one can \emph{prove} which inputs do, and which provably do not,
drive a model into an internal ``I am being tested'' state, and we built a working
answer for a small system by joining two prior ideas that had not been combined:
defining a concept by the text an activation-verbalizer emits (the NLA half) and
certifying reachability of that concept with a sound convex relaxation over a
one-hidden-layer ReLU network (the Raghunathan half). On cached GPT-2 the pipeline
runs end to end: it defines the eval-aware target $A^\star$, distils it into a
certifiable surrogate whose fidelity is measured rather than assumed, certifies
unreachability with a native, runtime-checked CROWN$(+$IBP) bound, enlarges the
certified radius by descending that differentiable bound, inverts to a real prompt
that crosses the threshold, and closes the loop to check that the crossing is
semantically real.

Two things are worth carrying forward. The machinery is real and the certificate is
genuinely sound --- every bound is validated against a brute-force witness, and the
tests pass. And the most robust finding is a cautionary one: a target defined by
geometry alone can be gamed by forcing a score across threshold far off the
activation manifold, where the surrogate says ``eval'' and the language readout says
``ordinary''; the loop-closure check catches exactly this. That negative result is,
we think, the part most likely to survive scrutiny, and it argues that
certificate-based and language-based checks are complementary rather than redundant.

We are equally clear about what the result is not. The task is synthetic and
near-separable, so most metrics saturate; certification is local, in activation space
through the surrogate, not back through the full transformer to tokens; the
verbalizer is a retrieval stand-in, not a trained NLA; and a reachability certificate
finds inference-time inputs that reach $A^\star$, not the training data that instilled
it. The four in-flight extensions --- a harder non-saturated task with error bars, a
deeper-layer certificate with CROWN/IBP ablations, the full off-manifold gaming sweep,
and a real open-model NLA --- are what would turn this validated skeleton into honest
science. The contribution of this paper is the skeleton itself: a concrete way to put
``define a concept in language'' and ``certify whether it is reachable'' in the same
loop, with the soundness and the stand-ins labelled at every step.
